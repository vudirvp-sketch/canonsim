# Peoples and Anomalous Biology

**Status:** active working canon.

This document owns biological lineages, lifecycle constraints, migration,
inter-lineage contact and the rules for introducing anomalous biology.

## 1. First rule

> **Do not build a race catalogue. Build distinct ways of living.**

A new lineage must change the world's causal possibilities through biology,
ecology, lifecycle, mobility, reproduction, sensory world, or another real
constraint.

## 2. Origin categories

The source corpus permits four broad origins for intelligent lineages:

- **Natural** — emerged through ordinary evolution and ecology.
- **Altered** — deliberately or accidentally modified.
- **Constructed** — intentionally built.
- **Emergent** — arose from a process whose agency/organization became complex
enough to support personhood.

No origin category is currently universal; individual lineages can remain
historically ambiguous.

## 3. Current core peoples

### Humans — WORKING

Humans are comparatively mobile, demographically flexible and culturally
adaptive. Their strengths are borrowing, mixing, migration and relatively rapid
institutional change.

Their costs can include shorter planning horizons, faster loss of accumulated
craft knowledge and weaker dependence on any single long-lived infrastructure
tradition.

### Sylvan lineage — WORKING

A long-lived forest-adapted lineage whose biology is compatible with living
systems and long ecological cycles.

Possible pressure profile:

- long lifespan;
- slower population turnover;
- strong biological/eco-system literacy;
- regeneration or symbiotic traits;
- weaker incentives for rapid industrial standardization.

The lineage should not map to one culture.

### Mountain lineage — WORKING

A lineage adapted to cold, vertical and/or confined environments, with strong
compatibility with complex structural infrastructure.

Possible pressure profile:

- confinement and hypoxia tolerance;
- high spatial/structural sensitivity;
- dense skeletal or thermal adaptations;
- long apprenticeships and maintenance-focused institutions.

The familiar "dwarven" pattern is not the canon; the environmental/biological
constraint is the canon candidate.

### Deepwater lineage — WORKING

A lineage or long-adapted population capable of life in deep aquatic habitats.

The pressure comes from pressure, light, moisture, food, energy, temperature,
transport and habitat integrity. Adaptation to the deep environment should make
surface life costly in return.

### Construct persons — WORKING

Constructs can be physical systems, ancient infrastructure descendants or
artificial bodies. Their defining problem is not appearance but continuity,
maintenance, purpose and personhood.

### Distributed persons — DEFERRED

The world can eventually support identities distributed across linked systems,
but this remains a late-stage possibility rather than an early-world population
assumption.

## 4. Biological asymmetry must have a cost

Any unusual trait follows:

`WHY DOES IT EXIST? → WHAT DOES IT COST? → WHAT DOES IT CHANGE? → WHAT DOES IT PREVENT? → WHAT CULTURE BUILDS AROUND IT?`

Examples:

- long life changes inheritance and apprenticeship;
- habitat adaptation creates housing constraints;
- slow reproduction changes war/recovery strategy;
- resonance dependence can make a lineage dependent on ancient infrastructure;
- unusual sensory abilities change work and social norms rather than simply
  granting an RPG bonus.

## 5. Resonance-dependent biology

### WORKING

Some living populations may depend on a local Resonance regime or ancient
engineered ecology.

If the supporting environment disappears, the lineage may deteriorate over time.
This creates a high-value historical conflict because preservation of the
infrastructure becomes preservation of life.

That pattern may apply to forest ecosystems, underwater habitats or other
regional systems.

## 6. Migration

Movement should follow actual biological and infrastructural constraints.

Possible patterns:

- humans migrate comparatively easily;
- sylvans move slowly and may follow habitat/ecology;
- mountain populations migrate with viable infrastructure;
- deepwater populations move where suitable habitats exist;
- constructs may move as systems rather than individuals.

These are authoring tendencies, not fixed demographic equations.

## 7. Inter-lineage contact

### HARD

The world permits:

- mixed cities;
- trade districts;
- diasporas;
- mixed families;
- cross-lineage professions;
- shared religions;
- intermarriage where biologically possible;
- prejudice, conflict and borrowing.

Species separation is not the default world structure.

## 8. Biology versus culture

Biology should usually affect **ordinary constraints** before it affects personality.

For example, a deepwater person in a surface port may need humidity control,
special housing and different temperature management. Those conditions shape
work, class, family and access without determining the person's character.

Likewise, a long-lived lineage can have conservative and radical cultures at the
same time.

## 9. Reproduction and demography

### DEFERRED until a region needs it

Exact reproductive cycles matter because they change simulation outcomes, but the
world does not need definitive demographic math before an authored population
actually depends on it.

The first required questions are:

- maturation time;
- reproductive constraints;
- population recovery rate;
- dependency on habitat or infrastructure;
- how family and inheritance respond.

## 10. Languages and religion

Different lineages do not require completely separate languages or pantheons.
Possible structures include shared trade languages, lineage languages, ritual
registers and technical vocabularies.

Shared religions across lineages are allowed and can be important to keeping the
world historically interconnected.

## 11. Rejected patterns for now

### REJECTED as default world structure

- classic playable "monster races" without their own systemic niche;
- undead as a mandatory separate species;
- demons/angels as a second cosmological substrate;
- dragons as a standard playable lineage;
- hive populations added merely for variety.

This is not a permanent ban. The gate is simple: a candidate returns only when a
real world-system reason makes the lineage necessary.

## 12. Undead

Undead are currently better treated as a **state or continuity problem** than as
a standard race category.

Possible states include preserved bodies, incomplete reconstructions,
Resonance-bound remains, autonomous residues and corrupted Echo.

This keeps the necropolis/continuity theme tied to the existing world model.

## 13. Working synthesis

The strongest lineage-level differences currently are:

| Lineage | Primary survival strategy |
|---|---|
| Human | adapt / migrate / borrow |
| Sylvan | continue living systems |
| Mountain | maintain complex structures |
| Deepwater | preserve habitat |
| Construct | preserve function / self |
| Distributed | preserve the network (late-game hypothesis) |

This is the reusable worldbuilding idea. The detailed physiology can remain
local until a region needs it.
