# World Kernel

**Status:** active working canon.

This document is the single owner of world-level identity and load-bearing
setting laws. Domain detail lives in the domain owner documents.

## 1. World identity

This is one world that experienced the collapse of an ancient, highly connected
civilizational network. The modern world is heterogeneous because different
societies answered the post-collapse problem differently: preserving,
localizing, adapting, forgetting, or rebuilding parts of the same inherited
reality.

The intended feeling is:

> **one history that diverged into many modes of living.**

The world is not a pile of unrelated genre regions. Different regions may feel
very different while remaining causally connected through geography, resources,
history, technology, institutions, culture, knowledge, and risk.

## 2. Central contradiction

**CONNECTIVITY ↔ RESILIENCE**

Connection enables exchange, knowledge, trade, scale, infrastructure and
technological growth. The same connection creates dependency, coordination cost,
shared failure points and cascade risk.

Fragmentation gives local resilience, autonomy and cultural divergence. The same
fragmentation causes knowledge loss, expensive exchange, incompatible standards
and weak ability to solve shared problems.

Neither side is the world's final answer. The setting's strongest conflicts come
from choosing which cost a society is willing to pay.

## 3. Second thematic axis

**CONTINUITY ↔ IDENTITY**

The setting repeatedly asks:

> What has to remain for something to count as continuing to exist?

The question applies to:

`person → culture → city → civilization → artificial mind → infrastructure → world`.

The world does **not** currently decide whether a soul exists, whether an
objective afterlife exists, or whether restored consciousness is numerically the
same person. Those questions remain open; see `LIFE_PERSONHOOD.md`.

## 4. Fundamental reality

### HARD — one physical substrate

Ordinary physics exists. There are not separate supernatural physics engines
for different peoples.

### HARD — Resonance is a property of reality

Resonance is a property of suitably related systems. It is not a universal
mana pool and not a separate substance. Its detailed owner is `RESONANCE.md`.

### HARD — world divergence is causal

Civilization is derived from:

`GEOGRAPHY + RESOURCE BASE + HISTORY + TECHNOLOGY + INSTITUTIONS + CULTURE + KNOWLEDGE + RISK + PATH DEPENDENCE`.

A biome, aesthetic or species label alone is never a sufficient explanation for
a civilization.

## 5. World pillars

### A — The Cost of Connection

More connection creates more possibility and more systemic coupling.

### B — The Price of Continuity

Keeping a life, structure, culture, memory or institution alive requires a
carrier, knowledge, energy, infrastructure, maintenance or another real cost.

### C — Residue of the Past

Important events remain in material structures, biology, institutions,
language, memory, law, myths and infrastructure. Past events are not just lore;
they alter current options.

### D — Uneven Civilization

Very different technological and cultural regimes can coexist when differences
are supported by geography, path dependence, knowledge, infrastructure,
switching costs, resource constraints and risk.

### E — Human Scale

Macro forces must reach ordinary life: work, housing, food, money, debt, family,
profession, law, love, conflict, memory, status and death.

## 6. Negative space

The following are rejected as universal world solutions:

- free or unlimited magic;
- unrestricted resurrection;
- perfect duplication or identity transfer;
- universal mind reading;
- absolute future knowledge;
- frictionless scaling of local technology;
- arbitrary mass teleportation;
- independent supernatural physics for each lineage;
- automatic technological convergence;
- lineage as a direct substitute for culture;
- history that leaves no present-day consequence.

The absence of these conveniences is part of world identity because each removed
shortcut forces institutions, tradeoffs and culture to exist.

## 7. Ancient world

### WORKING — Ancient Network

The ancient world is better modeled as a **civilizational network** than a single
empire. It contained multiple political and cultural centers linked by standards,
transport, trade, research, shared infrastructure and Resonance practices.

Its important achievement was not infinite power. It was the ability to connect
local systems into larger infrastructure.

The exact political structure, number of major centers and full network topology
remain open.

## 8. The Great Fracture

### WORKING — historical backbone

The current causal model is:

`high connectivity → systemic failure/cascade → infrastructure loss → fragmentation → path dependence → regional divergence`.

The Great Fracture should explain, together:

- the modern map;
- surviving and lost technologies;
- migration;
- ecological/hydrological change;
- cultural divergence;
- archaeological ruins;
- why reconnection is attractive;
- why reconnection is dangerous.

The exact mechanism of the initial network failure is not fixed. See
`WORLD_WORKPLAN.md` for the upstream question.

## 9. Historical backbone

The source corpus converges on a sequence of functions rather than a final list
of canonical names:

1. network expansion and integration;
2. political competition over network control;
3. first major Resonance or infrastructure cascade;
4. Great Fracture and loss of interregional interchangeability;
5. migrations and demographic redistribution;
6. ecological/hydrological transformation, including flooded zones;
7. long-term knowledge fragmentation;
8. regionally distinct reconstruction/industrialization;
9. renewed contact and partial reconnection.

The final historical chronology may rename or merge events, but it should keep
these causal jobs intact.

## 10. Technology law

For any high-impact capability distinguish:

`CAPABILITY → AVAILABILITY → REPRODUCIBILITY → ADOPTION → UBIQUITY`.

A technology is not globally common merely because it is physically possible.
A strong capability needs a bottleneck: materials, knowledge, infrastructure,
skilled personnel, ecology, capital, legal access, maintenance or another
specific dependency.

## 11. Culture law

**Lineage ≠ Culture ≠ Civilization ≠ Faction ≠ Person.**

Biology creates constraints. Environment creates pressures. History creates path
dependence. Culture turns repeated solutions into norms. Institutions make those
norms durable. Individual people still vary within all of them.

Culture should be legible through names, vocabulary, prohibitions, rituals,
property rules, family structure, work, food, time, law, religion, technology,
knowledge, memory and ordinary behavior.

The owner is `CULTURES_CIVILIZATION.md`.

## 12. Knowledge law

Important facts can exist simultaneously as:

`objective → perceived → known → inferred → believed → narrated`.

Different people may hold incompatible beliefs about the same event without the
world itself containing contradictory objective facts.

Knowledge asymmetry is a source of world depth, not a loophole for invention.

## 13. History law

A consequential event should be capable of leaving a chain such as:

`EVENT → OBSERVABLE → STATE CHANGE → KNOWLEDGE → SOCIAL RESPONSE → RESIDUE → NEW OPTIONS`.

Not every event needs every link. The point is that major history must remain
causally connected to the present.

## 14. Current scope

### HARD

- one physical substrate;
- Resonance as a common phenomenon;
- cost and limitation over miracle convenience;
- ancient high connectivity as the source of later divergence;
- geography as a causal driver;
- history as persistent residue;
- culture as learned constraint rather than race label;
- human-scale consequences as the quality frontier.

### WORKING

- Ancient Network as the pre-Fracture civilizational form;
- Great Fracture as a systemic collapse rather than a single simple battle;
- a family of regionally different civilization regimes;
- a later reconnection era.

### DEFERRED / MYSTERY

- exact soul ontology;
- definitive afterlife;
- exact ancient network architecture;
- the final chronology and naming of all historical breaks;
- distributed personhood at early-world scale;
- FTL/cosmic settlement as a current-era fact;
- a single "master mystery" that explains everything.

## 15. Current authoring frontier

The foundation is strong enough that adding more continental lore has diminishing
value. The next frontier is the **meso and meaning layer**: how macro pressure
becomes ordinary choices, relationships, institutions, and culturally specific
interpretations in one living region.

`ANCHOR_REGION.md` is the current laboratory.

## 16. One-paragraph formula

> Canonsim is one world shaped by the remains of an ancient, highly connected
> civilization. The modern world is diverse because its peoples inherited
> different environments, resources, histories, technologies, institutions and
> fragments of knowledge after the collapse of that network. Resonance is a
> common property of reality that can preserve, bind, transfer or otherwise
> leverage already possible structures, but it never grants free scale, free
> energy, perfect information, perfect restoration or guaranteed identity.
> The central tension is connectivity versus resilience; the second axis is
> continuity versus identity. World depth comes from persistent consequences,
> asymmetric knowledge, culturally specific constraints, and the human-scale
> lives produced by those pressures.
