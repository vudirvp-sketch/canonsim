# Life and Personhood

**Status:** active working canon.

This document owns the setting's life, death, Echo, personhood, continuity and
artificial-subject questions.

## 1. Life

### HARD — life is not information

A stored genome, memory, voice recording, behavior model or neural pattern is
not automatically a living organism.

### HARD — life is process

A living entity is defined by ongoing causal organization: maintaining itself,
interacting with its environment, adapting, and continuing its internal process.
The exact implementation can differ by lineage.

Therefore:

`same matter ≠ same life`,

and partial material replacement can coexist with genuine biological continuity.

## 2. Death

Death is the loss of the subject's capacity for continuous self-maintenance and
causal self-organization.

A useful progression is:

`living process → failure → decoupling → information loss → residual structure`.

The residual structure is where Echo becomes important.

## 3. Echo

Echo is the structural residue of a prior process.

It can survive in:

- biological tissue;
- physical changes to an environment;
- habitual behavior;
- recordings;
- objects;
- architecture;
- other people's memories;
- Resonance-compatible structures.

### HARD

**Echo is not automatically a soul.**

Echo, simulation, reconstruction and personhood are distinct categories.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Echo | residue of prior structure/process |
| Simulation | a model that reproduces behavior |
| Reconstruction | an attempt to rebuild prior organization |
| Person | an ongoing subject with agency and continuity |

## 4. Personhood

### WORKING definition

A person is a subject capable of maintaining a self-model, persistent
preferences, goals/intentions, agency, memory and causal participation in the
world.

Personhood therefore does not require biology.

A construct, artificial mind or distributed subject may be a person if it meets
the relevant conditions. The setting does not need one biological definition of
personhood.

## 5. Continuity and copying

### WORKING hypothesis

Personal identity is strongly tied to continuity of the causal process rather
than uninterrupted material identity.

A complete copy can begin with the same memories, preferences and history as an
original. At the moment trajectories diverge, the copy becomes a distinct
subject under this working model.

This does not settle every philosophical or legal dispute inside the world.

## 6. Resurrection

The setting permits partial or extensive reconstruction when enough structure,
information, material and infrastructure survive.

It does **not** guarantee that the reconstructed subject is numerically the same
person.

That creates a productive world-level disagreement:

- continuity-based cultures may accept a restored subject;
- body-based cultures may reject it;
- legal systems may define identity contractually;
- religious groups may invoke a soul doctrine;
- scientific communities may treat it as unresolved.

The facts can be shared while interpretations differ.

## 7. Soul

### MYSTERY

Three internally compatible interpretations remain available:

1. soul is a real but incompletely understood phenomenon;
2. soul is a cultural interpretation of continuity/personhood;
3. soul is an emergent property of certain living processes.

The world should not prematurely choose among them.

Even if a soul exists, Echo does not automatically equal soul.

## 8. Constructs and artificial minds

### WORKING

Ancient constructs can outlive the civilization that built them. They may
preserve function, memory, identity claims, or independent goals.

Not every construct is a person. The authoring question is whether a given
construct develops the properties relevant to agency and continuity.

Artificial minds are possible within the world model, but their persistence is
never free. Memory substrate, energy, maintenance, environment and social/legal
recognition can all become limiting factors.

## 9. Biological species versus personhood

`species` is a biological/lineage category.

`person` is a subject category.

The world may therefore contain:

- biological persons;
- constructed persons;
- artificial persons;
- possibly distributed persons later.

This separation is load-bearing because it prevents species design from carrying
all of the world's philosophical work.

## 10. Time and longevity

Longevity is not only a biological bonus. A major difference in lifespan changes:

- apprenticeship;
- memory depth;
- family structure;
- inheritance;
- risk tolerance;
- institutional turnover;
- willingness to invest in long projects.

A long-lived lineage should therefore have different temporal economics, not just
more hit points.

## 11. Worldbuilding rule

Whenever a setting element deals with life, ask:

`What continues? What is lost? What remains as residue? Who recognizes the survivor as the same?`

Those questions should produce institutions, disputes and ordinary practices,
not only metaphysical exposition.

## 12. Deferred questions

- exact biological definition for every possible edge case;
- legal status of every artificial subject;
- whether souls can be directly observed;
- whether a distributed person can remain a single identity across distance;
- definitive afterlife mechanics.

These become active only when a region or story family genuinely needs them.
