**What it is.** Paradox's grand-strategy space 4X (2016), whose
economy is **abstraction-first**: production and trade are aggregate
counters and route edges, not simulated agents — the polar opposite of
X4, and the deliberate design.

**Concrete mechanics.**

- **Strategic resources as counters.** A planet's deposits and a
  colony's jobs produce *units per month* of minerals, energy
  credits, alloys, consumer goods, rare resources. Everything is a
  counter; a deficit drains a stockpile and then applies a scripted
  penalty (resource shortage penalties, e.g. minerals → slow
  mining/construction). **No stock variable inside a production
  chain** — the chain is one arithmetic step.
- **Trade value and trade routes.** Trade value (TV) is generated by
  pops, flows along declared routes to a capital/starbase, is
  collected, and converts to energy credits. Routes are *edges the
  player draws* over owned systems; a starbase's collection radius and
  the trade capital's multiplier shape the flow. TV on a segment is
  sum-aggregated per segment — an edge carries a number.
- **Piracy as edge state.** Each unpatrolled route segment accumulates
  *piracy risk* per month (from TV magnitude); risk spawns pirate
  fleets when it crosses a threshold; patrolling ships suppress it.
  **The threat lives ON the edge, as accumulating state** — a dynamic
  edge weight, exactly the concept's §8.2 shape.
- **The market.** A galactic market with base prices and a sliding
  fee; buying pushes the internal price up, selling pushes it down
  (a dampened mean-reversion around a base). Internal market per
  empire before the galactic one unlocks. No per-station price
  differentiation — the abstraction is global by design.
- **Jobs and strata as consumption.** Pops fill jobs (miner,
  metallurgist); jobs are recipe rows (inputs upkeep → outputs);
  unemployment and strata demotion produce *happiness* pressure, not
  production failure — the social and physical layers are separate
  levers.

**What we take.** **The aggregate edge-state carrier**: res-1's flows
are exactly Stellaris trade segments — an edge carrying a number, with
*threat accumulating on the edge itself*. Our version: a route
(province road, river segment) carries a flow value + a risk state
(banditry pressure) that the director's world-side predicates read
(the detour fence law, `phases.md` §6); the risk is a fold over
events, never a stored mutable world-state object (STATE-1/D-006 —
ours stays derived). The **scripted-shortage penalty** is the
pack-law shape: scarcity's consequence is declared data (what drains,
what penalizes), not emergent arithmetic — res-1's sinks via the
irreversible item laws read this directly.

**What we adapt.** The market's mean-reversion is **refused** as a
mechanism (a second engine beside the canon door — D-116's fence);
what we keep is its *posture*: prices exist to be read, not to clear.
Trade-route *drawing* becomes pack-authored routes (the authored
exits win; roads-1 derives the generated half). Piracy's
threshold-spawn becomes a seeded consequence hook (TIME-1 SEEDED —
the weather-1 family), never a per-tick spawn roll.

**What inspires us.** Aggregate is enough: Stellaris runs a galaxy on
counters and proves the *drama* of scarcity lives in the flow
topology (chokepoints, exposure, collection), not in per-agent
simulation — the LOD principle's economy-side confirmation.

**Strengths.** The cheapest readable scarcity surface in the genre —
an overlay map answers "where is the value flowing and where is it
exposed" at a glance; our read surface copies that legibility (one
rendered line per flow). The edge-state threat is mechanically clean:
accumulation + threshold + countermeasure, three data rows. Designers
ship it: every Paradox modder can author economy rules in flat
scripting — the pack-data posture at scale.

**Weaknesses.** The global market flattens geography (one price per
empire/galaxy — no regional spreads, so distance risk never prices
in; the anti-shape for our river artery where the spread IS the
drama). Pops-as-counters strips the knowledge layer entirely — no NPC
knows about scarcity, hoards, or lies; our scarcity is *social*
(rumordrift, fidelity decay) as well as physical. Late-game counter
sprawl (dozens of resources) is the mono-resource minus inverted: Dune
row's law — ONE focal scarcity + ordinary flows; Stellaris forgot the
focus.

**Verdict.** res-1's **aggregate-edge donor**: flow-as-number,
threat-as-edge-state, consequences-as-declared-penalties — the
abstraction half of the scarcity cycle; paired with X4's stock-chain
cascades they cover the res-1 design space (aggregate drama +
mechanical failure).
