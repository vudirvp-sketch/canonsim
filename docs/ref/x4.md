**What it is.** Egosoft's space-sim (2018) whose headline system is a
**fully simulated production economy**: every station, factory ship,
and trade convoy is an NPC agent with a production rule and a cargo
hold, not a scripted backdrop — the "living universe" the series
sells.

**Concrete mechanics.**

- **Wares and production chains.** Every resource is a *ware* with a
  production recipe: inputs → outputs at a fixed cycle time. The food
  chain (energy cells → wheat → food) and the tech chains (energy
  cells + silicon → refined silicon → microchips; hull parts) are the
  canonical examples. A station declares its recipe and a *storage*
  per ware; production halts when an input store empties, resumes when
  it refills. **Production is local and stock-driven, never global.**
- **The NPC logistic loop.** NPC ships buy at stations with a surplus
  (price below the ware's economy-average), sell where a shortage
  raised the price, and repeat. Freighters are assigned to *trade
  ranges* (routine destinations), miners to resource fields, builders
  to construction sites. Every visible ship is doing this — no
  cosmetic traffic.
- **Dynamic prices from stock levels.** The price of a ware at a
  station is a function of its local storage relative to the station's
  declared capacity (the well-known buy-low/sell-high bands: cheap at
  >~70% full, expensive at <~30%). Prices are **derived from a state
  variable (stock), not negotiated** — a lookup, not a market.
- **Cascading failures.** The famous emergent behavior: destroy or
  starve one link (lose the energy-cell freighters in a war zone) and
  the chain degrades upward — refineries halt, hull-parts lines halt,
  shipyards stop building, the region's patrols thin, the war goes
  worse, more freighters are lost. A multi-step cascade from ONE
  removed input, carried by stock-outs, over in-game days.
- **Station building by agents.** NPCs (and the player) build new
  stations where projected margins are high: the economy grows its own
  capacity in response to scarcity — supply responds to price, slowly
  (construction time is the damping).

**What we take.** The **dependency graph as pack data** — res-1's
backbone: nodes (sources, flows, sinks) with recipes and capacities,
the graph itself declared in pack JSON, never a second engine. The
**stock-driven price** exactly matches our law: price spreads are
*derived read-side values* over a state variable — X4's
price-as-lookup confirms "never a negotiated market" is not a
simplification but a design. The **cascade** is the X4 row's whole
point for us: `convoy loss → local halt → price shock` is the phase-6
province's drama engine (the iron chain narrowing).

**What we adapt.** Scale and tick: X4 simulates thousands of ships in
real time; we aggregate flows as macro-events on the maclock cadence
(the closed cycle: source → flow → sink as pack data, D-116) — the
cascade survives aggregation because it lives in the stock variables,
not in per-ship pathing. The freighter's *trade range* becomes the
declared route (travel's edge + the convoy as a cold aggregate that
condenses when the reader's zone warms). NPC station-building is
**refused** — capacity growth on our side is authored pack content or
faction dynamics (depth-6), never an autonomous construction agent.

**What inspires us.** One removed input, days of consequence: the
desperation economy's shape (Kenshi's row) already named it — X4 is
the mechanical proof that scarcity cascades carry a story without a
script.

**Strengths.** The only mainstream game where the whole economy is
agent-built and agent-run (Distant Worlds shares the posture; X4's
sim is denser). Failure is legible: the player can trace WHY the
shipyard stopped by following the chain one link at a time — exactly
the readable causality our cause tree renders. The stock/price model
is fully deterministic and cheap to compute — it fits our maclock
cadence with room to spare.

**Weaknesses.** The sim's fidelity makes the late game opaque anyway
(hundreds of stations, no causal summary view — the UI never answers
"why is X scarce" in one line; our read surface: one rendered line
over integer truth, the Darkest Dungeon abstraction law). Performance
cost is real — Egosoft spent years optimizing the traffic; our
aggregate form pays nothing comparable. NPC trade logic is
deliberately myopic (no futures, no hedging) — fine at their scale,
but it means the *social* layer of scarcity (rumors, hoarding,
coercion) is absent; that half is ours (the knowledge/faction
systems), not theirs.

**Verdict.** res-1's **dependency-graph backbone**: the pack-declared
recipe graph, stock variables, and derived spreads — X4 is the
strongest single donor for "scarcity as a mechanical cascade, not a
scripted event", and the confirmation that price-as-derived-read is
the honest shape.
